Net Working Capital Summary

Westmill is a project-based business with favorable terms — 30% deposit at order and funds in place before vendors are paid — and carries no debt. Customer deposits (deferred revenue) fund roughly 60% of the receivables-and-inventory investment, so net working capital absorbs only about 6.8% of sales after deposits, well below the 15–25% typical of capital-equipment manufacturers.

Key Takeaways

Q2 (Oct–Dec) carried the year. November alone delivered $5.96M sales and $2.28M net income (38.3% margin) — a major equipment delivery quarter. Q2 represents nearly half of full-year sales at 48.2%.
Full-year forecast: $16.88M sales, $4.17M pre-tax net income (24.7% margin). Sales finish at 112.5% of the $15.0M budget; operating expenses land at 88.9% of budget, both favorable.
June absorbs the year-end OpEx catch-up. The forecast loads ~$736K of operating expense into the final period — roughly double a normal month — pulling June to a planned operating loss and stepping full-year operating profit down to $4.15M from the $4.44M May YTD peak. This is expected year-end accrual timing, not a deterioration in the business.
Net income held pre-tax. The $4.17M figure carries the 11-month below-the-line items with no incremental June tax accrual. The year-end tax true-up remains to be finalized and will reduce the after-tax figure.
Working capital is structurally light and customer-funded. Net working capital after deposits averages just $1.12M (6.8% of sales). With 30% paid at order, funds secured before vendors are paid, and no debt, incremental projects are largely self-funding — growth does not consume cash the way it does for most capital-equipment manufacturers.
